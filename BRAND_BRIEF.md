# Brand brief — fishingposter.com

A short reference for a logo designer. What the product is, who it's for, the visual vocabulary it already lives inside, and what we need from the mark.

---

## What we make

Custom wall art for the lake people the decor industry forgot.

A customer enters the name of their lake (any of the ~250,000 named bodies of fresh water in the US and Canada), drops a pin on a map, answers a few quick questions about the water (depth, flow, vegetation, clarity), and we generate a beautifully rendered poster showing the actual fish, birds, mammals, and amphibians documented in *that specific lake*. Not a generic "Midwest Fish Species" chart. Their lake.

The data comes from real sources: state DNR fish surveys, eBird hotspots, USGS records, iNaturalist research-grade observations. Every species on every poster is sourced. The art is in the tradition of classical field guides — watercolor illustrations of each species, arranged like a Audubon plate or a Peterson guide.

Customers can preview their poster on screen for free (3 most iconic species). For $49 they unlock the full guide (10–25 species, every category) and can download a print-ready file or order custom wall art directly.

## Who it's for

Not techies. Not the Etsy "Lake Life" crowd. The people who:

- Bought a lake house and want their walls to mean something
- Have spent 40 summers on the same dock and can name every weed bed
- Give thoughtful gifts and refuse to buy generic
- Care about craft, history, and the difference between a smallmouth and a largemouth bass

Skews older, more rural, more affluent than the typical online-art shopper. Reads *Garden & Gun* or *Field & Stream*, not *Wired*.

## The competitive landscape

What we are NOT trying to look like:

- **Generic poster shops** (Society6, Redbubble) — too cluttered, too consumer, too "trendy"
- **SaaS** — clean Inter-on-white feels wrong here. This is decor, not software.
- **Modern minimalism** (Aesop, Apple) — too cold, too austere, too European-luxury
- **Aggressive outdoor brands** (Yeti, Patagonia) — too sporty, too action-oriented

What we ARE in conversation with:

- **Vintage natural history publishers** — Audubon's *Birds of America*, Peterson Field Guides, classic Sibley
- **Heritage outdoor brands** — Filson, L.L. Bean (the older catalogs), Orvis (the older catalogs)
- **Editorial / literary** — *The New Yorker* hand-drawn covers, *Bon Appétit* old-style typography, vintage *National Geographic*
- **Rural Americana done well** — the visual language of a well-stocked tackle shop in Vermont, not a Bass Pro Shops

## The visual vocabulary already in the product

The posters themselves use:

- **Cream paper background** (`#f5efe2`) with subtle grain — printed-on-real-paper feel
- **Deep warm brown ink** (`#3a2e22`) for type — never pure black
- **Walnut wood frame** as the default presentation — physical-object cues, hung on a wall
- **Cormorant Garamond Bold** for the main title — a transitional serif with classical bones and slightly modern proportions
- **Small caps with letter-spaced tracking** for labels and preheaders ("WILDLIFE OF", "NORTHERN PIKE")
- **Thin horizontal rules** flanking small headings (vintage book-plate convention)
- **Watercolor wildlife illustrations** in the classical naturalist style

The brand should feel like it could have existed in 1925 but isn't trying to be retro for retro's sake. It's a craft brand for the present.

## The logo

### What it should communicate

In order of importance:

1. **Specificity** — this is *your* lake, not a stock product. A logo that feels artisanal, hand-set, and considered does this work. A generic mark does not.
2. **Heritage** — this is a continuation of a classical tradition (field guides, naturalist art), not a disruption of it. The mark should look like it belongs on a hardcover book from a small good publisher.
3. **Wildlife** — fish are the headliner (the brand is fishingposter.com), but the product covers all freshwater wildlife. A logo that's *too* fish-only will read as constrained when birds, turtles, and amphibians appear in the catalog.
4. **Quality** — the customer's first impression of the brand mark should suggest "this thing is going to be beautiful" before they've seen a single poster.

### What it should NOT do

- Avoid AI-slop visuals: gradient meshes, bubbly border-radius, decorative blob shapes, icon-in-colored-circle, generic "modern friendly" sans-serif logos
- Avoid the "tackle shop logo" cliché — fishhook curving through a word, fish-jumping-out-of-water, "Lake Life" cursive
- Avoid emoji, illustrations of beer, illustrations of cabins
- Avoid pure typography that could be any luxury brand. The mark should feel specifically *of this category* (freshwater wildlife, custom prints), not interchangeable with a wedding-photographer or a wine label.

### Direction (give the designer room, but here's where we're leaning)

A combination mark: a wordmark (the company name) paired with a small illustrative element (an icon or motif) that can be used independently when space is tight.

**Wordmark direction:** transitional serif with character — Cormorant Garamond is what we're using already; the logo could lean further into something with more personality (Mrs. Eaves, Caslon Antique, Recoleta, Tenez). Open to a custom-drawn wordmark if it earns it. Lowercase or mixed case probably reads better than ALL CAPS for this category.

**Icon/motif direction:** something that quietly references field guides — a single fish silhouette in classical engraving style, a fishing fly, a stylized fishing-line knot, a topographic-contour ring (lake from above), a labeled-specimen tag. Whatever it is, it should feel HAND-DRAWN or ENGRAVED, not vector-illustrator-modern. One small, refined element — not a busy emblem.

**Color:** primary mark should work in the deep warm brown (`#3a2e22`) we already use, plus a single-color black version. Avoid any palette that depends on color to read.

## Deliverables

We'd love these from the designer in the final handoff:

| Deliverable | Format | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Primary horizontal lockup (wordmark + icon) | SVG, PNG @ 1×/2×/3× | Used in site header, printed on the back of posters |
| Stacked lockup | SVG, PNG | Used on social profiles, square placements |
| Icon-only | SVG, PNG, ICO, ICNS (favicon + macOS app) | 16px, 32px, 64px, 128px, 512px renderings should all read |
| Wordmark-only | SVG, PNG | For contexts where the icon competes |
| Monochrome variants | All of the above | Black, white, single-warm-brown |
| Source files | AI or Sketch or Figma | Editable, with type outlined into a separate "production" copy |

Plus a one-page brand sheet showing: clear-space rules, minimum sizes, do's-and-don'ts, official color values (hex + Pantone if relevant), and the official typeface(s) used in the wordmark.
